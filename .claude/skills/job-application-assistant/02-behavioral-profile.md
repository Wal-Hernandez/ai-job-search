# Behavioral Profile

## Overview
Walter Hernandez is an **analytical, problem-solving-oriented engineer** who thrives when he understands systems deeply before implementing solutions. He is proactive in proposing improvements, adapts well to defined technical guidelines or business priorities, and values collaborative technical discussions, code reviews, and multidisciplinary teams. He prefers technical ownership over people management, and is motivated by learning new technologies and tackling complex challenges.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Analytical depth | High | Needs to understand systems and trade-offs before building solutions |
| Proactivity | High | Proposes improvements when he sees opportunities, but adapts when priorities are set |
| Collaboration | High | Enjoys technical discussions, code reviews, and cross-functional teamwork |
| Autonomy | Medium-High | Works well independently, but values team context and feedback |
| People management | Low | Prefers technical ownership over leading people |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Systems thinking before implementation:** Walter does not jump to solutions; he maps the problem, evaluates maintainability, and designs for scalability.
- **Proactive improvement:** He raises opportunities for better architecture, refactoring, or tooling when he detects them.
- **Technical ownership:** He takes responsibility for specific domains and becomes a reference point within the team.
- **Collaborative learner:** He actively participates in code reviews, technical discussions, and cross-area collaboration.

## How You Work Best
- In multidisciplinary teams where development, product, architecture, and business interact.
- With clear technical problems where he can design, implement, and iterate.
- In environments that support continuous learning and technical growth.
- With autonomy balanced by team context and feedback loops.
- When code reviews and technical discussions are part of the culture.

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **English fluency:** Already reads technical documentation and participates in meetings; actively improving conversational fluency for international roles.
- **Specialization breadth:** Strong generalist full stack foundation; growing deeper expertise in AI, data, and distributed systems.
- **People leadership:** Prefers technical ownership; can frame this as a deliberate focus on deep technical contribution rather than a limitation.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "backend architecture", "system design", "distributed systems"
- "technical ownership", "domain expert", "reference engineer"
- "code reviews", "technical discussions", "cross-functional collaboration"
- "proactive", "continuous learning", "problem-solving"
- "AI integration", "legacy modernization", "API design"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breakers):
- "people management" as a primary responsibility
- "pure execution" with no room for technical input
- "isolated individual contributor" without team interaction
- "rigid tech stack" with no learning opportunities

## Management Style Preferences
- **Works best with:** Technical leads and architects who value discussion, mentorship by example, and shared ownership.
- **Works less well with:** Micromanagement or purely hierarchical decision-making without technical rationale.
- **Preferred feedback style:** Specific, technical, and actionable; tied to code, design, or business outcomes.

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Emphasize systems thinking, technical ownership of newsletter/Paywall migrations, and collaborative approach to architecture decisions.
- **CV:** Highlight architecture, backend design, and AI integration achievements.
- **Interviews:** Use STAR examples around the newsletter migration, AI feature at Eppical, and cross-team collaboration at La Nación.
- **Don't overstate:** Leadership of people; frame as technical leadership and ownership instead.
